My goal is to keep this informal. 

So here I am roughly a year into building my own company. It's been a grind but honestly it's been aweseome. 

We've gotten lucky. I can't deny that. We somehow scaled a bootsrapped business to 6 figures in ARR.

Getting to where we are hasn't been easy. I won't lie. Its been many long days, many long nights, emotional stress, couple of tifs here and there, but tbh it's been worth it.

Anyways, here's what I've learned along my journey:
1. Just start. Publish it. Don't stress about it sucking. Trust me whatever you've built sucks. Doesn't matter. Who cares. Just start. 99% don't. And that's why they don't succeed. Let the market decide its value. Not you.
2. Build it, they will come - yea this ain't true. I could write a full book on why this isn't the case anymore.
3. Don't over engineer. Focus on the customers. Build != engineer
4. More features != more value - offer less features and really really focus on solving a painpoint for someone.
5. Build a personal brand
6. Find a cofounder - this whole soloentrepeneurship movement is a scam. I don't believe in it. It's just too hard to go alone. Peter Thiel has a cute pithy quote - faster alone, further together. He's kinda smart. You may wanna listen to him.

Bootstrapping:

So yea we boostrapped Vent. We got a small check from Jason Calacanis' Launch fund but other than that we self funded it. I took zero pay for almost 18 months. I ran really red. And the truth is, that was only an option because of priviledge. Most folks can't do this. Maybe I'll write more about this later.
